# Seed listening scripts (original examples)

All scripts are original, written for this plugin as format references. They
are NOT from any official exam. In a live drill: give the learner the
questions first, then deliver the script (TTS if available, otherwise the
learner reads it once at natural speed or has someone read it aloud), then
collect answers before revealing the key.

## Everyday dialogue + form completion (IELTS Part 1 style, B1–B2)

**Questions (complete the booking form, one word/number per gap):**
1. Class type: ____ for beginners
2. Day: ____ evenings
3. Start time: ____
4. Price per term: £ ____

**Script:**
> **Receptionist:** Riverside Community Centre, good afternoon.
> **Caller:** Hi — I'm ringing about evening classes. Do you still run the
> pottery class for beginners?
> **Receptionist:** We do. That one meets on Thursday evenings — it used to
> be Tuesdays, but we moved it this term.
> **Caller:** Thursday works. What time does it start?
> **Receptionist:** Half past six, and it runs for ninety minutes.
> **Caller:** And the cost?
> **Receptionist:** It's ninety-five pounds for the term, materials included.

**Key:** 1 pottery · 2 Thursday · 3 6:30 (half past six) · 4 95

## Mini-lecture + multiple choice (IELTS Part 4 style, B2–C1; also the shape of a TOEFL academic talk)

One speaker. A TOEFL academic talk runs up to 250 words (about 170–200 in
the official practice test; this one is shorter) and carries **4
questions** — typically the topic, a detail, why the speaker mentions
something, and an inference or what comes next. An IELTS Part 4 lecture is
longer and uses completion items instead; the script below serves both.

**Questions:**
1. What is the talk mainly about?
   A. Why canals replaced roads in the 1790s · B. The problems of building a
   canal tunnel ✅ · C. How candles were made for miners · D. The cost of
   lining a tunnel
2. According to the speaker, early canal builders lined tunnels with clay
   mainly to…
   A. strengthen the walls · B. keep water from escaping ✅ · C. smooth the
   surface for boats · D. protect the diggers
3. Why does the speaker mention candle flames?
   A. To show how crews checked that air was moving ✅ · B. To explain how
   the workface was lit · C. To describe signals between crews · D. To show
   how slowly the work went
4. What can be inferred about the two digging teams?
   A. They finished much later than planned · B. They measured their
   direction very carefully ✅ · C. They started from the same end · D. They
   used different methods

**Script:**
> When the Marlton canal tunnel was cut in the 1790s, engineers faced two
> quiet enemies: water and air. Water first — the tunnel passed through
> porous sandstone, so the diggers lined every metre with puddled clay, not
> to hold the roof up, as people often assume, but to stop the canal simply
> draining away into the rock. Air was the subtler problem. With shafts only
> every half mile, crews judged ventilation by candle flame: a flame leaning
> steadily meant air was moving; a still flame meant danger, and on the
> worst days work simply stopped until the wind changed. What impresses
> surveyors today, though, is none of this — it's that two teams digging
> from opposite ends met in the middle barely a hand's width out of line.
> Nobody is quite sure how they managed it with ropes, plumb lines and
> little else.

**Key:** 1 B · 2 B · 3 A · 4 B

## Short conversation + 2 questions (TOEFL iBT 2026 format)

Two speakers, about 50–90 words, heard once. The setting is everyday, a
workplace or a campus — not only a campus. The two questions usually ask
what a speaker means or implies, why someone did something, or what one
speaker suggests. Label the speakers by role or as Man/Woman.

**Script:**
> **Woman:** Hi, I'm calling about the washing machine in flat six. It
> stopped in the middle of a cycle again.
> **Man:** Again? I thought the engineer replaced the pump last month.
> **Woman:** He did, but now the door won't open, and my clothes are still
> inside.
> **Man:** All right. Don't force it. Switch it off at the wall, wait ten
> minutes, and the lock should release. I'll send someone tomorrow morning.
> **Woman:** Tomorrow's fine, as long as it's before nine.

**Questions:**
1. Why is the woman calling?
   A. To ask for a new washing machine · B. To report a machine that has
   stopped working ✅ · C. To complain about the engineer · D. To change the
   time of a visit
2. What does the man suggest the woman do?
   A. Pull the door open · B. Call the engineer herself · C. Turn the power
   off and wait ✅ · D. Start the cycle again

**Key:** 1 B · 2 C

## Announcement + 2 questions (TOEFL iBT 2026 format)

One speaker, about 45–70 words, heard once: a classroom or campus
announcement. Say where it is heard ("an announcement in a student
lounge"). The two questions usually ask what it is about (or its purpose)
and what listeners should do.

**Script (an announcement in a university library):**
> Attention, please. The third-floor study area will close at five o'clock
> today so that the new lighting can be installed. If you are working there,
> please move to the first-floor reading room, where extra tables have been
> set up. Books left on the third floor will be taken to the front desk. The
> area will reopen on Monday morning. Thank you for your patience.

**Questions:**
1. What is the main purpose of the announcement?
   A. To introduce a new reading room · B. To explain why part of the
   library is closing early ✅ · C. To ask students to return their books ·
   D. To announce new opening hours for the whole library
2. What should students who are using the third floor do?
   A. Leave the library before five · B. Collect their books on Monday ·
   C. Wait at the front desk · D. Move to the first floor ✅

**Key:** 1 B · 2 D

## Listen and Choose a Response (TOEFL iBT 2026 format)

Each item is one short utterance — a question or a remark of a few seconds,
at most about six stressed syllables — heard **once**, with no transcript;
the learner picks the best reply from four printed options. A module of the
test opens with a run of these (8 in the official practice test). Deliver
the prompts one at a time — see `references/listening.md` for the per-item
playback protocol. Label each prompt *Man:* or *Woman:* and vary the voice.

**Distractor design (each wrong option should fail in a different way):**
one option echoes words from the prompt but ignores its function; one answers
a question that was not asked; one is topically related but pragmatically
irrelevant; one contradicts something the prompt implied.

> 1. *Heard:* "Would you mind if I opened the window?"
>    A. No, go ahead. ✅ · B. Yes, I opened it. · C. It's on the third floor. ·
>    D. I don't mind windows.
>    *(Tests the "Would you mind" trap: "No" grants permission. B answers a
>    different question, C is topical noise, D echoes "mind" mechanically.)*
> 2. *Heard:* "I've been trying to reach Professor Aldridge all week."
>    A. Have you tried her office hours? ✅ · B. She reached me on Tuesday. ·
>    C. All week is a long time to teach. · D. Yes, I've been reaching.
> 3. *Heard:* "Do you happen to know when the lab reopens?"
>    A. Not offhand — the noticeboard might say. ✅ · B. Yes, I happen to. ·
>    C. The lab is quite large. · D. It opened last year.
>    *(B is grammatical but unnatural: it answers the literal form, not the
>    request for information.)*
> 4. *Heard:* "That's the third time the system has logged me out."
>    A. Try clearing your browser cache. ✅ · B. Congratulations. ·
>    C. The system was built in 2019. · D. You should log out more often.
> 5. *Heard:* "I'd have come to the seminar, but my train was cancelled."
>    A. That's a shame — I'll send you my notes. ✅ ·
>    B. Why did you cancel the train? · C. The seminar is on Thursdays. ·
>    D. So you were there after all?
>    *(Tests the counterfactual: "I'd have come, but…" means the speaker did
>    NOT come. D contradicts that; B misreads who cancelled what.)*

**Key:** 1 A · 2 A · 3 A · 4 A · 5 A
*(Shuffle the option order when generating a real drill — the correct answer
must not always be A.)*
